Civics test audio: 128 questions, read aloud, with the answers

The civics test is an oral exam. You will not be handed a page of options — an officer says a question and waits for you to speak. The only way to practise that is to hear the question and answer it out loud. This is the whole 2025 list as audio: question, six seconds of silence for your answer, then the acceptable answers.

Why the questions are grouped by theme, not by difficulty

The USCIS list is already grouped: the first 72 questions are American government, the next 46 are history, and the last 10 are symbols and holidays. Studying in that order builds a structure you can fall back on in the interview: if you blank on "what does the judicial branch do", you still have the three-branch picture in your head from the previous twenty questions. Random order is for the practice tests, not for learning.

The six-second pause

Each question is followed by six seconds of silence. That is longer than you need for "George Washington" and shorter than you need for "name two cabinet-level positions" — deliberately, because the interview does not wait for you to compose a perfect sentence. Answer immediately, even badly, then listen to the model answer. Two passes through a file is usually enough for the short answers; the "name two" and "name three" questions need three or four.

Ten minutes a day, or a whole commute

  • One file per day: seven days covers the bank once. Re-listen only to the file where you missed the most.
  • Before the interview: play Theme 2 (system of government, 47 questions) at random and answer out loud. It is the densest theme and the one where a hesitation costs the most.
  • With the PDF open: write down the numbers you miss; the PDF has the full answer list for each one, including the alternative answers the audio summarises.

Voice and speed

One en-US voice at slightly slower than conversational pace, so that the words you will need to pronounce in the interview — legislative, judicial, emancipation, sovereignty — are clear. If it is still too fast, most podcast apps can slow playback to 0.8x without changing pitch.

Questions that have no fixed answer

President, Vice President, Speaker of the House and Chief Justice change with elections and appointments; your two senators, your representative, your governor and your state capital depend on where you live. In the audio these eight questions say so instead of giving a name, and point you to uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates. We do not read out a name that could be wrong on your interview day.
