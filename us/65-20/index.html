The 65/20 exception: 20 questions, 10 asked, 6 to pass

Applicants who are 65 or older and have been lawful permanent residents for 20 years or more on the date they file are tested on a reduced set: the 20 questions marked with an asterisk in the USCIS list. The officer asks 10 of them and 6 correct answers is a pass. You may also take the civics test in the language of your choice.

The English language requirement has separate age rules that are often confused with this one: no English test if you are over 50 and have been a permanent resident for 20 years, or over 55 with 15 years. The 65/20 civics exception is different and requires the 20-year figure with age 65+.

How to study 20 questions in a week

  1. Day 1: read all 20 questions and answers once, aloud.
  2. Days 2 to 4: listen to one audio file a day — the starred questions are spread across all seven files, so use the list above as your checklist and tick each one you answer without hesitating.
  3. Day 5: write the 20 answers from memory. Any question you cannot write, you cannot say under pressure either.
  4. Day 6: have someone ask you 10 random questions from the 20. Count your correct answers. Below 6, repeat day 5.
  5. Day 7: a second round of 10, then stop. Cramming the night before adds anxiety, not recall.

Two of the 20 change with elections (the President and the Speaker) and one depends on your state (your governor). That is 3 of 20 — enough to fail on its own if you guess wrong. Check them the day before.
